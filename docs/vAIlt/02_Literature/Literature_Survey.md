# Literature Survey

Synthesis of the 12 papers in `docs/lit_survey/noise_addition/markdown/`, organized by theme, for the robust-test-set work built on K-Radar. Per-paper detail is in the linked notes; this note states what each contributes, where they disagree, and the gap this project sits in.

## Theme 1 — Corruption benchmarks (parameter-level sensor/point-cloud noise)

[[CommonCorruption_PointCloud_Benchmark]] and [[BenchmarkingRobustness_MSF]] are the two papers this project's own Gaussian Noise and Loss Partial corruptions are directly sourced from. Both define **25 / 14 corruption types respectively** with explicit severity-swept numeric parameters (σ, drop fraction, dB, mm/hr) rather than opaque "low/medium/high" labels — the same design principle this project's [[04_Design/Noise_Taxonomy]] follows. [[LiRTest]] is the LiDAR-specific precedent with its own independently-derived rain/snow/fog attenuation model (coefficients `a, b` per weather type, swept weather-strength `W`).

**Where they disagree**: CommonCorruption finds radial/coordinate Gaussian noise the natural per-point corruption model for LiDAR; BenchmarkingRobustness defines Gaussian noise identically for camera pixels *and* LiDAR coordinates without giving numeric σ (its exact values must be cross-referenced from CommonCorruption or independently calibrated); LiRTest's snow model adds synthetic noise *points* (σ=8, vehicle-proximal) on top of intensity attenuation — a materially different mechanism from CommonCorruption's pure coordinate-noise formulation, and neither paper reconciles the two. **This project's own radar corruption (`noise_induced_shifts`, replacing Gaussian noise for radar specifically) is a third, distinct choice** — modeling radar corruption as a per-point coordinate shift rather than intensity noise, on the stated grounds that radar corruption is physically a coordinate-domain effect. None of the three source papers make this radar-specific distinction explicitly; it is this project's own synthesis, not directly traceable to one paper. **Open gap, not resolved by any surveyed paper: no consensus severity-scale mapping exists across these three noise models** — a σ=0.05m LiDAR Gaussian corruption in one paper is not obviously equivalent in real-world severity to a W=10 rain setting in another.

## Theme 2 — Metamorphic / mutation testing methodology

[[LiRTest]] (LiDAR, affine + weather transforms), [[MOT_Robustness_Metamorphic_Testing]] (MOT, frame-deletion/freeze/pixelate/blur MRs — the direct source for this project's Frame Deletion implementation), [[MetaOD_Object_Insertion]] (camera, compositional object insertion), and [[Semantic_Mutation_Testing_LiDAR]] (LiDAR, semantic entity-level mutation) form a coherent methodological thread: **all four solve the oracle problem** (no ground-truth relabeling needed) by defining a metamorphic relation the model output must satisfy under transformation, rather than requiring hand-labeled corrupted data.

**Where they disagree**: Semantic_Mutation_Testing_LiDAR explicitly critiques weather-simulation approaches (LiRTest's own family) as "realistic but low-diversity" because they don't change the semantic label, and critiques generic augmentation noise as "diversity-increasing but not realism-verified" — a direct methodological tension between the two testing philosophies (physically-parameterized sensor noise vs. semantically-valid compositional mutation) that this literature set does not resolve; they're better read as complementary axes than competing approaches. MOT_Robustness's finding that **noise-type corruption (pixelation/blur) hurts tracking more than information-loss corruption (deletion/freeze)** is a useful prior for this project when deciding which corruption category to prioritize for evaluation depth.

## Theme 3 — Fusion robustness (multi-sensor failure/degradation)

[[BenchmarkingRobustness_MSF]] (7 camera-LiDAR MSF systems, complete/partial signal loss + spatial/temporal misalignment), [[RCBEVDet]] (radar-camera BEV fusion, complete-sensor-dropout robustness), and [[SeeingThroughFog]] (real multi-sensor fog/snow/rain, entropy-gated fusion) converge on one finding stated three different ways: **naive fusion architectures do not degrade gracefully under single-sensor failure** — BenchmarkingRobustness shows complete LiDAR loss crashes all 7 systems it tests; SeeingThroughFog shows naive camera-LiDAR fusion baselines fall *below* image-only performance in dense fog; RCBEVDet shows a fixed-weight fusion baseline (CRN) degrades far faster under radar dropout than its own cross-attention design. **All three papers independently conclude that the fusion *mechanism* (deep/cross-attention vs. late/concatenation) determines robustness more than which sensors are present** — directly relevant to this project's `primary_sensor`/`num_sensors_visible` design (see [[04_Design/Output_Schema]]), which currently uses unweighted argmax score comparison with no explicit fusion-mechanism robustness modeling.

**Where they disagree**: RCBEVDet finds radar-camera fusion robust to *radar* dropout specifically via cross-attention; SeeingThroughFog finds radar's apparent weather-robustness is partly a **confound from simplified adverse-weather scenes** (fewer/farther objects), a caution the fusion-mechanism papers don't cross-check against. This project's own visibility-score work — grounded in per-object, per-frame ground truth from [[KRadar_Dataset_Paper]] rather than aggregate weather-category AP — is positioned to test this confound directly, which none of the three fusion papers can do with their coarser per-scene metrics.

## Theme 4 — Sensor/dataset foundations

[[KITTI_Vision_Benchmark_Suite]] establishes benchmark-design principles (diversity-maximizing sampling, calibrated multi-sensor ground truth, AOS-style orientation metrics) that every later paper in this survey inherits, but **explicitly excluded adverse-weather scenes** from its own curation — the historical starting point this whole literature set exists to correct. [[KRadar_Dataset_Paper]] is the dataset this entire project is built on: its central RTNH-vs-PointPillars result (radar AP stable, LiDAR AP drops 22–30 points under sleet/heavy snow, same scenes/objects) is the empirical anchor every theoretical claim about radar weather-robustness in [[03_Theory/Sensor_Noise_and_Degradation_Models]] ultimately traces back to. [[4D_mmWave_Radar_Adverse_Environments_Survey]] situates K-Radar within ~20 comparable weather-inclusive radar datasets, flagging K-Radar's lack of long-range point clouds as its main documented weakness. [[TMVA4D_Radar_Semantic_Segmentation]] is the outlier in this set — real (not simulated) dust/smoke/water-spray degradation in field-robotics settings, not automotive weather — included because it's an independent empirical data point for radar particulate-robustness, with the explicit caveat that its degradation mechanism (particulate scattering) differs physically from the precipitation attenuation this project's radar work actually concerns.

## The gap this project fills

No surveyed paper combines: (1) K-Radar's real, per-object, per-weather-category ground truth, (2) a physically-parameterized, severity-swept corruption suite in the style of [[CommonCorruption_PointCloud_Benchmark]]/[[BenchmarkingRobustness_MSF]], and (3) a continuous, per-sensor **visibility score** (not just binary present/absent or scene-level AP) as the unit of analysis. The closest analogs either use real weather but only scene-level metrics ([[KRadar_Dataset_Paper]], [[SeeingThroughFog]]), or use synthetic corruption with rich severity parameters but no per-object visibility grounding ([[CommonCorruption_PointCloud_Benchmark]], [[LiRTest]]). This project's [[03_Theory/Visibility_Formulas|visibility pipeline]] plus its noise-injection work is positioned to close that gap — but the **unresolved cross-paper severity-scale consistency problem** (Theme 1) and the **radar-robustness-vs-scene-simplification confound** (Theme 3) are both open methodological risks inherited from this literature, not yet independently verified within this project.
