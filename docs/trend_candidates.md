### 1. LiDAR Gaussian noise causes a step-function mAP collapse, not a graded one
- **Comparison**: `lidar_gaussian_noise_{low,medium,high}` mAP, Pass 1, collapsed over context.
- **Effect**: low → 68.4 (−6% vs. 72.9 baseline), medium → 23.7 (−67%), high → 21.6 (−70%). Low severity is nearly harmless; medium is already near-total collapse; medium→high barely worsens further.
- **N**: 39,926 / 36,630 / 36,297 — all well above threshold.
- **Supporting**: `pass1_condition_main_effects.csv`; `pass4_distance/mAP_lidar.png`, `pass4_frame_density/mAP_lidar.png` (collapse visible at every distance/density bucket, not concentrated at range extremes).
- **Confidence**: high. No other sensor/effect shows this cliff shape — camera and radar degrade roughly monotonically and mildly by comparison (radar high-severity gaussian: 72.9→54.0, a much shallower slope).

### 2. The LiDAR collapse's failure mode is missed detections, not spurious ones — and FP rate actually *drops* under it
- **Comparison**: FN_rate vs. FP_rate, `lidar_gaussian_noise_high` vs. `original`.
- **Effect**: FN_rate 0.234 → 0.841 (+260%); FP_rate 0.153 → 0.130 (a *decrease*). Same pattern holds for `radar_gaussian_noise_high` (FN 0.234→0.415, FP 0.153→0.104) and `lidar_loss_complete_zero` (FN→0.542, FP→0.189, the one exception where FP rises).
- Across all 22 conditions, FP_rate never exceeds 0.19 while FN_rate ranges up to 0.84 — corruption in this sweep suppresses detection confidence/output rather than manufacturing phantom objects.
- **N**: full condition N (36k–40k per condition).
- **Supporting**: `pass1_condition_main_effects.csv` (FP_rate/FN_rate columns).
- **Confidence**: high, consistent direction across every corrupted condition.

### 3. mAOE stays flat even when mAP collapses — orientation of *found* objects doesn't degrade, only detection count does
- **Comparison**: mAOE across all 22 conditions, Pass 1.
- **Effect**: range is narrow (0.10–0.22 rad) with no correlation to mAP — `lidar_gaussian_noise_high` (mAP 21.6) has mAOE 0.137, actually *lower* than `original`'s 0.207. mATE and mASE similarly move much less than mAP's collapse would suggest (mATE 0.326→0.466, a 43% increase vs. mAP's 70% drop).
- **Interpretation**: corruption in this sweep operates almost entirely through the detector's TP/FN decision boundary, not through the localization/orientation quality of whatever it does detect.
- **Supporting**: `pass1_condition_main_effects.csv`.
- **Confidence**: medium — plausible mechanism, but worth checking whether this is a property of the ASF pipeline specifically or a general detector behavior before treating it as a thesis claim.

### 4. Environment and weather main effects dwarf most corruption effects — `alleyway`/`university`/`rain` are hard regardless of condition
- **Comparison**: Pass 2 context main effects (condition collapsed).
- **Effect**: environment mAP ranges from 27.1 (`alleyway`) / 31.7 (`university`) to 95.0 (`shoulder`) / 76.0 (`urban`) — a 68-point spread from scene context alone, versus corruption's largest single-condition drop of ~51 points (`lidar_gaussian_noise_high`). Weather: `rain` mAP 32.3 vs. `fog` mAP 89.9 — fog is the *easiest* weather condition here, not the hardest, which is counter-intuitive and worth sanity-checking against the underlying scene composition (rain sequences may skew toward inherently harder environments/ranges rather than rain itself being the causal factor — this table can't separate the two, only a controlled/matched comparison could).
- Also notable: mAOE spikes sharply in `countryside` (0.869), `parkinglots` (0.878), and `university` (0.500) versus ~0.08–0.24 elsewhere — an orientation-specific weak spot independent of corruption.
- **N**: smallest environment bucket is `countryside` (16,629) / smallest weather bucket `overcast` (29,520) — both comfortably above threshold at the Pass-2 level.
- **Supporting**: `pass2_context_main_effects_environment.csv`, `pass2_context_main_effects_weather.csv`.
- **Confidence**: high on the existence of large baseline effects; low on attributing `rain`'s difficulty to rain itself without controlling for confounded scene composition — flagged as a methodology caveat, not a clean finding.

### 5. LiDAR-noise collapse is context-modulated: mildest in rain/alleyway/shoulder, worst in parkinglots/overcast/mountain — churn confirms it, not just mAP
- **Comparison**: Pass 3 condition×context, `lidar_gaussian_noise_{medium,high}` vs. `original`'s own bucket baseline — now the precomputed `interaction_delta` column (cell mAP − that same bucket's `original` mAP), not the flawed `mAP_delta_from_main_effect` column (see methodology note below; this finding's numbers were re-verified against the corrected column on 2026-08-28 and one part corrected as a result — see next line).
- **Effect (mAP, weather)**: `interaction_delta` for `lidar_gaussian_noise_{medium,high}` ranges from ≈−32 to −33 in `rain` (the *least* damaged bucket) and ≈−42 in `fog`, up to ≈−53 to −62 in `overcast`/`sleet`/`normal`. **Correction**: earlier drafts of this finding (computed by hand, before `interaction_delta` existed as a column) claimed fog was the most cushioning weather bucket — checking `rain` explicitly against the real column shows rain cushions the collapse even more (≈10 points less damage than fog). Re-verify any weather-specific claim against the column directly rather than trusting a partial hand-check.
- **Effect (mAP, environment)**: `interaction_delta` ranges from ≈−22 to −39 in `shoulder`/`alleyway`/`university` (mildest) up to ≈−69 in `parkinglots` (worst), with `mountain`/`highway` also severe (≈−53 to −60). `shoulder` and `alleyway` also happen to be the two most extreme *baseline*-mAP environments (95.0 and 27.1 respectively — finding #4), consistent with a floor/ceiling effect: baseline-easy or baseline-hard scenes both have less room for a corruption-specific swing than mid-difficulty scenes do.
- **Effect (churn)**: `lidar_gaussian_noise_high`'s churn_rate by environment ranges from 0.73 (`parkinglots`) / 0.71 (`university`) down to 0.21 (`shoulder`, N=866, low-N) and 0.39 (`alleyway`) — a >3x spread. This corroborates the mAP-based finding independently: `shoulder` and `alleyway` (the two environments with the most extreme *baseline* mAP, 95.0 and 27.1 respectively — see finding #4) both churn far less under this corruption than the mid-difficulty environments (`university`/`highway`/`parkinglots`/`mountain`/`urban`, all >0.60). One plausible read: baseline-easy scenes have redundant/high-margin detections that survive noise; baseline-hard scenes may already be past the point where corruption changes much (floor effect) — worth investigating further, not confirmed here. (Numbers corrected 2026-08-28: churn was originally computed at IoU=0.5 via an unchecked library default, `gta.DEFAULT_IOU_THRESHOLD` — inconsistent with `row_table_iou03.parquet`'s 0.3 used for every other metric here. Re-run at 0.3; ranking and conclusion unchanged, magnitudes shifted by roughly 1-6 points.)
- **N**: smallest cell in the mAP comparison, `lidar_gaussian_noise_high × parkinglots`, N=1039 — just above threshold. Churn's `shoulder` cell (N=866) and `countryside` cell (N=552) are flagged low-N — the churn-vs-baseline-difficulty pattern above is directionally consistent even excluding those two, but treat the extremes as provisional.
- **Supporting**: `pass3_condition_x_weather.csv`, `pass3_condition_x_environment.csv` — sort/filter by the `interaction_delta` column (cell mAP − same-bucket `original` mAP; added 2026-08-28). Do NOT use `mAP_delta_from_main_effect` for this — see the methodology note below.
- **Confidence**: medium-high. Two independent metrics (mAP double-difference, churn_rate) agree on direction and rough magnitude ordering.

### 6. Camera corruption barely moves mAP but produces a flat, severity-insensitive churn signal
- **Comparison**: `camera_*` conditions, mAP vs. churn_rate, Pass 1.
- **Effect**: mAP is essentially flat across all 7 camera conditions (70.3–71.5, vs. 72.9 baseline) including `camera_loss_complete_zero` (camera fully zeroed) — consistent with sed/bus detection being LiDAR/radar-dominated in this ASF pipeline. But churn_rate is nonzero and *also* flat (2.0%–2.8%, corrected 2026-08-28 from an IoU=0.5 miscomputation — see finding #5's note) regardless of severity or effect type — the same ~2% of GT objects flip TP↔FN even when the aggregate metric doesn't budge and even at the lowest camera-noise severity.
- **Interpretation**: aggregate mAP would suggest camera corruption is a non-issue; churn says otherwise — the same *magnitude* of per-object churn as camera severity increases, suggesting it may not be causally driven by corruption intensity at all (could be near the level of ordinary run-to-run detector noise / matching-threshold jitter rather than a genuine corruption effect) — worth a direct comparison against a repeated `original`-vs-`original` run if one exists, to establish a noise floor.
- **N**: churn `total` = 35,455 GT objects per condition (well above threshold).
- **Supporting**: `pass1_condition_main_effects.csv` (churn_rate column, merged via `merge_churn.py`).
- **Confidence**: medium — real signal, but needs a noise-floor baseline before treating the flatness as meaningful rather than measurement noise.

### 7. Highest churn tracks mAP collapse closely at the condition level — no clean mAP-stable/high-churn divergence found among the severe conditions
- Checked explicitly per the task's checklist item: `lidar_gaussian_noise_high` (mAP 21.6, churn_rate 0.609) and `_medium` (mAP 23.7, churn_rate 0.594) have both the worst mAP and the worst churn — net_retention 0.21/0.23 respectively. No condition combines near-baseline mAP with high churn *except* finding #6's camera pattern (flat-low mAP drop, flat-low-but-nonzero churn) and `lidar_loss_partial_medium` (mAP 73.0, mRR 1.002 — essentially at baseline — yet churn_rate 0.053, meaning ~5% of GT objects still flipped identity even though the aggregate metric net-cancelled). The `lidar_loss_partial_medium` case is the cleanest example of the checklist's "recovers as much as it costs" pattern in this sweep. (Numbers corrected 2026-08-28, see finding #5's IoU note.)
- **Supporting**: `pass1_condition_main_effects.csv`.
- **Confidence**: high on the observation; the "no strong divergence at the severe end" is itself informative (rules out a hypothesis, not just confirms one).

### 8. Radar churn scales monotonically with distance (range/SNR signature); LiDAR churn doesn't — it's already saturated
- **Comparison**: churn_rate vs. `distance_bin` (`[0,20)`/`[20,40)`/`[40,60)`/`[60,75)`, GT range), added 2026-08-28 via `run_churn_pass4.py` + `pass4_stratifier_charts.py`'s new `churn_rate` panels.
- **Effect**: `radar_gaussian_noise_high` churn_rate rises monotonically and steeply with distance — 0.103 (near, 0–20m) → 0.184 → 0.235 → 0.326 (very far, 60–75m), a ~3.2x increase. This is exactly the range/SNR-driven degradation the task's checklist flagged as a candidate mechanism to look for, and it shows up cleanly. `lidar_gaussian_noise_high`, by contrast, is flat across distance (0.589–0.636, no trend) — consistent with finding #1's step-function characterization: LiDAR's collapse is severity-driven and already near-saturated everywhere, so there's no further room for a distance gradient to show up.
- **Effect (frame_density)**: weaker/mixed — `radar_gaussian_noise_high` churn_rate *decreases* slightly with density (0.239 at 1 object → 0.170 at 4+), `lidar_gaussian_noise_high` *increases* slightly (0.484 → 0.641). Neither is as clean or as large as the distance effect; plausible but not pursued further here.
- (Numbers corrected 2026-08-28, see finding #5's IoU note — direction and shape of both trends unchanged.)
- **N**: smallest distance-bin cell (very_far, `radar_gaussian_noise_high`) N=4060 — well above threshold. All distance/density churn cells check out above 1000.
- **Supporting**: `outputs/robustness_reports/exploratory/pass4_distance/churn_rate_{camera,lidar,radar}.png`, `pass4_frame_density/churn_rate_{camera,lidar,radar}.png`.
- **Confidence**: high on the radar distance trend (clean monotonic, large N, matches a known physical mechanism); medium on the density finding (real but small, no obvious mechanism identified).
